6G Maritime Communication Breakthrough! The First Non-Stationary Multi-Mobility Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) Ship Channel Model Released
In the development process of the integrated 6G air, ground, and sea communication network, UAV communication in maritime scenarios, as an important application direction for mobile base stations, is becoming a research hotspot in the field of communication. The modeling of UAV ship channels is the core foundation for the design, testing, and performance evaluation of UAV communication systems in maritime scenarios. Recently, a team of IEEE scholars proposed a new non-stationary multi-mobility UAV ship channel model suitable for 6G maritime communication, filling the gap in high-precision UAV channel modeling for maritime scenarios. The related research results provide important theoretical support and technical references for the development of 6G maritime UAV communication systems.
What are the challenges in modeling UAV communication channels in maritime scenarios? Unlike land communication environments, UAV communication in maritime scenarios presents unique channel characteristics that pose multiple challenges to channel modeling:
Firstly, wave fluctuations on the sea surface produce irregular oscillations, causing scattering bodies to move in an irregular state, making multipath fading characteristics more complex;
Secondly, changes in oceanic climate can lead to signal attenuation, while factors such as humidity, pressure, and temperature in the marine atmospheric layer form waveguide effects, becoming an important propagation mechanism for UAV communication in maritime scenarios, directly altering channel characteristics;
Thirdly, existing channel models focus mainly on terrestrial UAV communication with ground stations, and standardized models (such as the 3GPP geometrical model) have not yet covered UAV communication with ships, and most maritime channel research focuses only on large-scale fading, simplifying analysis of small-scale fading characteristics and neglecting the coupling effect of wave fluctuations, waveguide effects, and multimobility.
Moreover, current maritime channel modeling methods also have significant limitations: Ray tracing does not consider the mobility of UAVs and ships, and has high computational complexity; Time-domain finite difference method (FDTD) struggles to integrate waveguide effects and cannot accurately depict the real propagation characteristics of maritime channels. Therefore, constructing a high-precision UAV ship channel model that fits the actual conditions of maritime scenarios and takes into account multiple dimensions of influencing factors becomes an urgent need for the development of 6G maritime communication.
Four major innovations, creating a specialized channel model for maritime scenarios. Addressing the above issues, the research team proposed a 3D non-stationary multi-mobility unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) for ship communication channel model, which is the first to achieve a comprehensive and accurate depiction of the characteristics of UAV communication channels in maritime scenarios. The core innovations are reflected in four aspects:
1. The first integration of multi-mobility, focusing on the small-scale fading characteristics model for the first time incorporates arbitrary speed and directional movement between drones, ships, and their scattering clusters, breaking through the limitation of traditional models that only consider single node mobility. At the same time, it focuses on studying the small-scale fading channel characteristics in maritime scenarios, filling the gaps in existing research and more accurately reflecting the dynamic realities of maritime drone communication.
2. Utilizing the sea surface fluctuation equation, the model precisely describes the evolution of scattering clusters. In response to the irregular sea surface fluctuations, the model incorporates a multi-directional irregular sea current summation model to depict sea surface fluctuations. It employs a single cluster model to describe the distribution of sea surface scattering bodies, and integrates the life-and-death process with the sea surface fluctuation equation, achieving precise descriptions of the generation, disappearance, and movement of scattering clusters in response to changes in the sea surface, thereby reproducing the dynamic characteristics of sea surface scattering.
3. Considering the effects of ocean waveguides, the dual-cluster model accurately depicts multipath propagation in marine scenarios, taking into account the evaporation waveguide (high probability below 40m sea surface) and elevated waveguides (600m-3000m). The waveguide effect is abstracted into a multi-order reflection process, using the dual-cluster model (Cₙ,ₐ and Cₙ,𝒵) to describe signal propagation under waveguide effects. Additionally, the impact of adverse weather conditions on signals is abstracted as virtual links, incorporated into the modeling of multi-hop (MB) components, fully covering the important propagation mechanisms in marine channels.
4. Full-parameter time-varying design, which aligns with the inherent model of non-stationary channels, sets all parameters such as drone, ship, scattering clusters' speed, acceleration, motion angles, and reflection order, waveguide size, etc., as time-varying parameters. This accurately captures the non-stationary characteristics of maritime drone channels, breaking through the applicability boundary of traditional stationary channel models, and more accurately conforms to the actual changes in maritime communication channels.
The model divides the communication link between unmanned aerial vehicles (UAV) and ships into three core components: Line of Sight (LoS), Single-Bounce (SB), and Multi-Bounce (MB). These three components are independent and collectively form the complete channel impulse response (CIR), which reconstructs the signal propagation path from UAV to ship in maritime scenarios from the physical layer.
1  
Line of Sight (LoS) component: The direct communication link between the drone and the ship, which is the fundamental path for signal propagation. The model precisely calculates the time-varying delay, Doppler frequency, and channel gain of this path.
2  
Single-Bounce (SB) component: The link from the signal reaching the ship after a single reflection on rough seas, triggered by sea surface fluctuations. The model describes the sea scattering bodies through a cluster model, calculates the time-varying distance, angle, and Doppler frequency between the scatter clusters and the transmitter/receiver, characterizing the multipath characteristics of single-bounce reflections on the sea surface.
3  
Multi-hop (MB) component: The signal, influenced by sea waveguide effects and adverse weather conditions, travels through multiple reflections before reaching the ship. The model introduces waveguide delay τ_wg(t) through a dual cluster model and multi-order reflection theory, accurately depicting the multi-hop propagation characteristics under waveguide effects.
The model rigorously derives the channel impulse response of three components through mathematical formulas, providing calculation methods for core indicators such as time-varying delay, Doppler frequency, channel gain, and angle parameters (including azimuth/elevation angles of transmission and reception). It also defines a complete parameter system (comprising over 40 key parameters), offering a rigorous theoretical basis for the model's simulation and engineering applications.
Seven major statistical characteristics, comprehensively verifying the scientificity of the model. To validate the accuracy and effectiveness of the model, the research team has deduced and analyzed seven typical statistical characteristics of maritime UAV channels based on this model. From multiple dimensions such as time domain, spatial domain, frequency domain, and delay domain, the model's scientific nature has been verified. At the same time, key changes in maritime channels have been revealed:
1. The time-correlation function (ACF) shows significant changes in its values over time, directly reflecting the non-stationarity of the channel; and the faster the drone/ship's moving speed and acceleration, the faster the ACF decays, indicating stronger node dynamics and weaker channel temporal correlation.
2. The spatial cross-correlation function (CCF) is influenced by antenna rotation angles and node mobility. It significantly changes with the distance between the receiving end antenna and time, and different antenna rotation angular speeds lead to different CCF trends, demonstrating the channel's spatial non-stationarity.
3. The Doppler power spectral density (PSD) directly affects the power distribution of Doppler frequency for drones. Different directions at the same speed or the same speed at different directions will result in different PSD trends, accurately capturing this characteristic.
4. The delay power spectral density (PSD) is significantly affected by waveguide size and reflection order. Larger waveguide size and higher reflection order lead to more complex power distribution in the delay domain, fully reproducing the effect of waveguide on signal delay distribution.
5. The angle power spectral density (PSD) presents the "birth and death" process of scattering clusters from the airspace, where cluster appearance and disappearance cause dynamic changes in the angle PSD, intuitively reflecting the dynamic evolution characteristics of sea surface scattering bodies.
6. The stable interval is calculated using a local stable region (LRS) method, which decreases as the drone speed increases; and the closer the drone is to the ship, the faster the angle parameter changes, further shortening the stable interval, providing reference for the sampling interval design of marine communication systems.
7. The root mean square (RMS) delay spread is larger when the reflection order is higher due to the waveguide effect. Through comparison with NASA Pacific Ocean C band real measurement data, this model's RMS delay spread highly matches the real data, while significantly differing from land drones that have a sparse ground channel (sea scene scattering body distribution is more sparse, resulting in smaller delay spread), verifying the practicality and uniqueness of the model.
Additionally, through the comparison of analytical results with simulation results, the study verified the correctness of the derivation of core statistical characteristics such as ACF and CCF, ensuring the theoretical rigor of the model.
The research significance and application value lie in empowering the development of 6G maritime communication. The proposed novel non-stationary multi-mobility UAV for ship channel model is a significant breakthrough in the field of 6G maritime communication channel modeling. Its research significance and application value are reflected in three aspects:
1. It fills a theoretical gap and improves the 6G channel model system by constructing the marine UAV for ship channel model that considers sea surface fluctuations, waveguide effects, and multi-mobility coupling, filling the coverage gap in existing 6G channel models for maritime scenarios, providing a new theoretical paradigm for integrated air-ground-sea communication network channel modeling.
2. It provides design basis for the research and development of maritime UAV communication systems. The deduction of seven statistical characteristics and key parameter patterns in the model provide precise theoretical basis for trajectory optimization, antenna design, modulation and demodulation scheme selection, link budget analysis, etc., significantly enhancing the scientificality and practicality of system design.
3. It lays the foundation for testing, promoting the standardization of maritime communication technology model. The real-time reproduction of dynamic maritime UAV communication scenarios can be achieved, providing a foundation for the effective testing of emerging drone for ship communication technology and network architecture, while also laying an important foundation for subsequent standardization of maritime communication channel models.
In summary, this study, by integrating core factors such as multi-mobility, sea surface fluctuations, and waveguide effects into the construction of the first 3D non-stationary UAV for ship channel model, achieves precise characterization and scientific analysis of the maritime UAV communication channel characteristics. The related achievements provide crucial channel modeling support for the landing application of 6G integrated air-ground-sea communication network in maritime scenarios.
In the future, as maritime drone communication expands into millimeter-wave and terahertz bands, and as complex scenarios such as multi-drone coordination and integrated ship-shore systems emerge, channel models will need to further integrate new factors such as wide-band characteristics, multi-node collaboration, and intelligent reflective surfaces. The modeling approach and methods proposed in this study provide an important reference and inspiration for subsequent channel modeling in complex maritime scenarios, driving the development of 6G maritime communication technology towards higher accuracy and more practical applications.
